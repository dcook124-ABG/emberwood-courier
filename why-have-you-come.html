Why Have You Come?

A Word from the Editor

Nobody walks up Champion's Way by accident. Somebody's owed, somebody's dead, or somebody's lying, and it's often you. Work out which, and bring the answer to your first session. The Editor would print your story if he could. Give him one worth printing.

A note from your DM. (This part is out of character.)

This campaign is about Drakkenheim. You will enjoy it most with a character who has a reason to be here and something at stake in the city, its people, or its history. That could be someone they lost, a side they took, a debt, a faith, or a fortune.

Below are the five backgrounds written for this city, each with three character ideas to borrow, bend, or steal. Turn a card over to see who that character already knows in Emberwood. I'll offer personal quests at our first session, so leave some room.

D
Magic and Gods in Drakkenheim
  • The gods do not answer. They don't appear, speak, or meddle.
  • Holy power is earned through devotion and resolve. Clerics, druids, and paladins are not handed it.
  • Arcane magic is in the blood. Sorcerers and wizards are born with it, raised by the Amethyst Academy, and barred by law from titles and land.
  • Magic is rare. About one person in a thousand can cast a spell at all.

Playing a bard, sorcerer, warlock, or wizard? The Mageborn background is written for you. There is more in The Wider World.

Five Lives That Lead Here

Continental Nobility

Some noble house in Caspia, Elyria, or Westemär had the misfortune of producing you. The Fall either ruined your family or handed it an opening, and you're here about one or the other. Remember that no one with arcane magic in the blood may hold a title. Your cousins certainly remember.Last of the Line: the only one left, without a copper to your name, come to dig the family back out of the rubble. Possibly by hand.

Open to any class but sorcerer, warlock, or wizard.

Ask YourselfWhich side did your house back in the war, and who still holds that against you?

The Spare

Your elder brother has the title, the lands, and the good chair by the fire, and you have a fine cape and a letter of introduction. Your house has been paying toward the reclaiming of this city for years, along with half the nobility of the realm, and someone at the family table finally asked what the money bought. You were sent to find out. Nobody asked if you minded.

The Spare

Who You Know
The Hooded Lanterns, whose bills your family helps pay. Ansom and Petra Lang at the Lantern Watchtower know your name, or at least your crest.
Plays Well As
A knight, a commander, or anyone who talks their way through a door before kicking it.
Still to Decide
What does your family want you to find here, and what would you rather find instead?

Last of the Line

There was a house, once, with your name over the gate and forty people under its roof. The Fall took the fortune, the war took the cousins, and time took the rest, until all that's left of a proud old line is a signet ring, a threadbare cape, and you. Everything your family owned in Drakkenheim is still in there. The Lanterns will tell you it belongs to the Crown now. There is no Crown.

Last of the Line

Who You Know
The Hooded Lanterns, who have posted their opinion of your inheritance on every door in camp. Kosta Stavros at the Red Lion Hotel, who remembers when your name was good for a room.
Plays Well As
A fighter with something to prove, a rogue with good manners, a paladin with nothing left to lose.
Still to Decide
What was your house known for, and is that a thing you want back?

The Heir by Default

You were never meant to inherit. Then your sister set the drapes alight at nine years old without touching them, the Academy came for her within the month, and the law struck her name from the line and wrote yours in. You have carried her title ever since like a borrowed coat. She came of age some years back. Mages go where the stone is, and all the stone is here.

The Heir by Default

Who You Know
The Amethyst Academy, in the person of their buyer at the Red Lion Hotel, who will be civil and tell you nothing. The Knights of the Silver Order at the Chapel, who have views on mages and will assume you share them.
Plays Well As
Any class but a sorcerer, warlock, or wizard. A noble who is easy around magic is a rare and useful thing.
Still to Decide
Do you want to find her, or only to know she doesn't want the title back?

Mageborn

Magic came in with your blood, and the Amethyst Academy came for you not long after. You're out from under them now, but never out from under the laws that bind every mage in the realms.Malfeasant Mage: expelled or self-taught, and very careful about who finds out which.

For bards, sorcerers, warlocks, and wizards.

Ask YourselfWhat did the Academy take from you when it took you in, and would they have you back?

The Graduate

You finished your apprenticeship, shook the proper hands, and walked out of the Academy a free mage with a clean record and no idea what to do next. The Academy keeps few of its own in the field and pays handsomely for stones and for careful notes. You know exactly what they want and exactly how they like it written up. You are not working for them. You are merely selling to them, often.

The Graduate

Who You Know
The Academy's buyer at the Red Lion Hotel, who may have taught you or been taught beside you. The Knights of the Silver Order, who will be perfectly polite and watch your hands.
Plays Well As
A wizard above all. Also a bard with a scholar's training, or a sorcerer who learned to pass for studious.
Still to Decide
What did you study, and what are you hoping the stone will tell you about it?

Malfeasant Mage

You do not wear the colors. Either the Academy showed you the door or you never knocked on it, and you learned what you know from a hedge teacher, a stolen book, or a long run of nearly fatal guesses. Common folk get nervous around a mage without a master. You have chosen to make camp a short walk from the Academy's buyer and the Order's chapel, which is either nerve or a plan.

Malfeasant Mage

Who You Know
Nobody, officially. Old Zoya at the Shrine of the Old Gods answers to neither the Academy nor the Flame. The back tables at the Skull & Sword don't ask where a talent came from.
Plays Well As
A warlock, a sorcerer, or a wizard with a great many gaps. Any mage who would rather be taken for something else.
Still to Decide
Were you thrown out or never taken in, and who in this camp could tell the difference by looking?

The Dreamer

It began the autumn the stars fell, when you were a child a long way from here. The same dream, every few weeks, of colors you had no names for and a sound like a wet finger on the rim of a glass. Your teachers called it nerves. Then you met a prospector with a stone in a lead box, and it was humming your dream. You packed that night.

The Dreamer

Who You Know
The Followers of the Falling Fire at Hendrix Farm, who would call your dream a summons and make room for you at the fire. The Amethyst Academy, who would call it a symptom and take notes.
Plays Well As
A sorcerer or a warlock. Anyone whose magic came to them unasked.
Still to Decide
What does the dream show you that you have never told a soul?

Devoted Missionary

Your faith sent you. Or a vision did. Or a prophecy you couldn't shake, however much you drank.Stargazer: you watched the star fall from a long way off, and something's had a hook in you ever since. Some nights you can feel it pull.

Ask YourselfWhat sacred purpose brought you here, and would the village chapel bless it?

The Envoy

Your temple chose you because you write a clear hand and do not frighten easily. The Faith of the Sacred Flame has split clean down the middle over this city, with one side calling the stone a blight and the other calling it a gift, and your superiors would like an honest account from someone who answers to neither camp. You have a small book, a great deal of ink, and both sides smiling at you.

The Envoy

Who You Know
Flamekeeper Hanna at the Chapel of Saint Ardenna, who will give you a bed. High Flamekeeper Ophelia Reed of the Silver Order and Nathaniel Flint of the Falling Fire, each of whom would like to see what you write.
Plays Well As
A cleric or a paladin. A monk or a learned bard would serve as well.
Still to Decide
Which side did you expect to favor when you set out, and has the road changed your mind?

Stargazer

You saw it. You were a long way off, on a hill or a ship's deck or a rooftop, and you watched a star come down out of a clear sky and put out the lights of a city. You have never been able to say what you felt, except that it had a direction. It took you years to walk here. You would be hard put to explain a single one of them.

Stargazer

Who You Know
The pilgrims at Hendrix Farm, who walked the same road and sing about it. You needn't hold with their prophet to share their supper.
Plays Well As
Anyone at all. A ranger, a druid, a monk, a warlock. The star was not particular.
Still to Decide
What did you leave behind to follow it, and does anyone there know where you went?

Keeper of the Old Ways

Yours is the Old Faith, the one with no cathedral, only the seasons and the stones and the people who went before. Word reached your circle of a valley where nothing will grow, not a turnip, not a weed, and where the pines are dying from the top down. That is not how land dies. Somebody ought to go and look at it properly. You drew the short straw, or you asked for it.

Keeper of the Old Ways

Who You Know
Old Zoya at the Shrine of the Old Gods, who keeps the standing stones outside the village and will not be pleased to see you. She may warm up. Bring something.
Plays Well As
A druid first of all. A ranger or a barbarian raised in the old ways fits just as well.
Still to Decide
What did your elders ask you to bring home: an answer, a warning, or a cure?

Survivor

You stayed when everybody with sense ran, and you know which streets will kill you, because you've watched them do it.Veteran of the Civil War: soldier or refugee, you walked away from the war. Not all of you did.

Ask YourselfWhat did you lose in those ruins, and what did you have to do to get out?

The One Who Stayed

You were a chandler's apprentice in the outer wards the night the sky came down, and when everybody with sense ran, you had nowhere to run to. So you stayed. Fifteen years you have lived on what the city drops, and you know which streets hum and which ones have gone quiet for a reason. The Lanterns pay for that kind of knowing. So do people who don't wear a uniform.

The One Who Stayed

Who You Know
The Hooded Lantern scouts at the Watchtower, who would hire you tomorrow. The back tables at the Skull & Sword, who made the same offer first.
Plays Well As
A rogue, a ranger, a scout of any stripe. Someone with quick feet and a thick pair of gloves.
Still to Decide
Why now? After fifteen years alone in there, what made you come out and take up with a crew?

Veteran of the Civil War

You marched for five years under one banner or the other, and you were good at it, which is not the same as being proud of it. Then both claimants died inside a week and the war just stopped, like a clock. Nobody paid you. Nobody thanked you. You went home and found you were no use there. Emberwood has work for people who are no use anywhere else.

Veteran of the Civil War

Who You Know
The Hooded Lanterns, who are what's left of the army and always short of sergeants. The veterans at the Skull & Sword, who will not ask which side you were on if you extend the same courtesy.
Plays Well As
A fighter, a ranger, a barbarian. A cleric who was a chaplain, or a bard who was a drummer.
Still to Decide
Which side did you march for, and what would you do if someone from the other one sat down at your table?

The Child of the Outer Wards

You were small when it happened. You remember being carried, and a light that threw the shadows the wrong way, and a door being shut. Everything else you know about that night you were told by the people who raised you on the road, and they did not always agree. You are grown now, and you have come back to see how much of what you remember is true.

The Child of the Outer Wards

Who You Know
Flamekeeper Hanna at the Chapel of Saint Ardenna, where notices for the missing are left and prayers for them are said. The gate sergeant at Shepherd's Gate, who takes down every name that goes in.
Plays Well As
Anyone young. This idea suits whatever you most want to play.
Still to Decide
Who carried you out, and who was on the other side of that door?

Treasure Seeker

Gold brought you, or relics, and you'd just as soon not say which. The Courier respects your privacy and will be watching you anyway.

Ask YourselfWhat is the one prize worth dying for, and who else wants it?

The Prospector

You have done the arithmetic. One stone the size of your finger is a year's wages. One the size of your fist is a farm. You own a lead box, a pair of tongs, and gloves in two thicknesses, and you have read every word the Market Desk ever printed. You are not here for glory, or the throne, or the good of the realm. You are here for six good months and then never again.

The Prospector

Who You Know
Armin Gainsbury, who sold you the box. Aldor the Immense and Orson Fairweather in Caravan Court, one of whom you mean to make like you.
Plays Well As
Anyone careful. A rogue, a ranger, anyone who reads the label first.
Still to Decide
What is the money for? Nobody does this sum for no reason.

Soldier of Fortune

Other people come for what is lying in the ruins. You come for what is walking around in them. There is good money in beasts and bandits, paid by the head, and Drakkenheim is the only place you know of where the supply has never once run short. You have a sharp eye for a wanted notice and a long memory for a face. You sleep very well.

Soldier of Fortune

Who You Know
The Hooded Lanterns, who post bounties and pay them. The Queen's Men at the Skull & Sword, who appear on a fair number of those notices and would like a quiet word.
Plays Well As
A fighter or a ranger. A paladin who takes payment, or a rogue who prefers the hunt to the lock.
Still to Decide
Is there one notice you would take for nothing?

The Gentleman of No Fixed Title

You have a signet ring, a letter of identification, and a very plausible manner, and not one of the three would survive a close look. It has never mattered. A camp full of strangers with money and no law is the finest thing that ever happened to you, and somewhere in that ruin there are rings and seals and letters that are the genuine article. A person could become somebody.

The Gentleman of No Fixed Title

Who You Know
Blackjack Mel at the back tables of the Skull & Sword, who spotted you inside a minute and has said nothing yet. Kosta Stavros at the Red Lion, who has not spotted you at all.
Plays Well As
A bard or a rogue. Anyone with a quick tongue and a deck of cards.
Still to Decide
Who were you before you were this, and who is still looking for that person?
