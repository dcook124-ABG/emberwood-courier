    What Is Waiting Past the Gate

    The Dark City

    Read this before you go in. Nobody reads it after.

    By Corvin Stray, Field Correspondent. Eleven trips in. Eleven out.

    In Short

    Drakkenheim lies under the Haze, a glittering mist you cannot rest in. Delerium crystals grow through the ruins; they are valuable, but touching them bare-handed makes you sick. Linger too long or handle the stones carelessly and you become contaminated, which only magic can cure. Five gates lead into the Inner City, and the Hooded Lanterns hold one of them.

    Here's what nobody tells you. It's beautiful. You come over the last rise and there it is, a whole city of spires and domes drowned in colored fog, violet and gold and a green like the inside of a wave, and the streets glittering up through it like somebody spilled the night sky. A hundred thousand people lived there. What lives there now is the Haze, the stones, and whatever the two of them have made of what got left behind. Fifteen years of scavenging has boiled the whole business down to five rules. Nobody who broke them has come back to argue.

    The Rules of the Road

    1. No rest in the Haze.Sleep in there and you'll wake as tired and torn up as you lay down. If you wake. Come out to heal. Then go back in.
    2. Never touch the stones bare-handed.Gloves. Tongs. A rag, if it comes to that. Those are your fingers, and you'll want all ten for counting the money.
    3. Know your way out before you go in.Retreat is a plan. Running blind is a funeral. Chalk your turns.
    4. Not every monster is meant for you.There are things up there that would eat your whole crew and pick their teeth with the pack frames. Nobody's paying you to fight them. Go around.
    5. Mind your company.Five outfits work the ruins. Every one of them is hiring, and every one keeps a tally of who it owes and who owes it. Know which column you're in.

    What the Survivors Say

    Dangers of the Dark City

    The Haze

    It rolled through the streets the night of the Fall, every color there is, and it has never had the decency to leave. It sits on the whole city like a lid on a pot and slops out over the walls. It does strange things to sleep and stranger things to spells, and worse to whoever stops too long. Fifteen years of very clever people have tried to keep it out of things. It's in all of them.

    The Stones

    Delerium. It comes up through the ruins like frost through a field, fused right into brick and cobble. It hums. Not loud. You feel it in your back teeth. It shines after dark, which helps you find it, and it will make you sick if you handle it the way you've handled everything else in your life. Bring tools. Bring lead.

    The Change

    Stay too long, or get careless with the stones, and the ruins go to work on you. Body first. Then mind. Your charms against poison and plague are so much jewelry against it, and no amount of sleep will touch it. Only magic turns it back. If your friend comes home wrong, you don't wait to see if it passes. You take them to the Chapel. You carry them if you have to.

    The Things Inside

    The dead up there don't all keep still. The rats and the dogs quit being rats and dogs a long time ago. Most of what will come at you down those streets used to pay taxes, and some of it still wears the hat.

    The Gates and the Walls

    There are five gates into the Inner City. The Hooded Lanterns hold one and talk big about the other four. Ask a scavenger who's holding the rest and watch the sudden interest they take in the bottom of their cup. As for going over the walls instead, you can try it. Once.

    The People

    Plenty of the living up there have nothing against you personally. They just want what's on your back. A crew staggering out heavy is a good deal easier to rob than a vault is to find. The Queen's Men worked that sum out years ago, and they've been collecting ever since.

    Corvin's Last Word: Count your crew at the gate going in. Count them again at the gate coming out. If the second number is bigger, run.
