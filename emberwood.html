    The Last Village on the Road

    Emberwood

    You walked the last five miles of Champion's Way expecting a graveyard. You are early.

    Emberwood was a market and a pub when the stars fell, and if you'd told the folk there what was coming they'd have asked you to finish your drink and go. The Fall put out every window in the place. The years after put out most of the families, as the fields went grey and the pines commenced dying. Two hundred mean, stubborn sons of bitches stayed, on the theory that it was theirs. Then the adventurers came up the road with coin in their boots, and the camp found out what it was for.

    It's for this: you go north, you come back if you're able, and between the one and the other you are relieved of every copper you own. Half the doors are nailed shut. What's worth finding is a short walk from the square, and what isn't will find you.

      A Word on Prices, Before You Complain

      How Things Work Here

      Nothing grows here. Not a turnip. Every nail, loaf, and bandage comes up that road on a wagon and whoever drove it wants paying, so you'll give double what you gave at home for steel and kit, and five times over for anything you mean to eat. Water goes by the cup, same as ale, and the ale's better. Go on and try to haggle. Folks have. The shopkeep will tell you to take your trade elsewhere, then stand and enjoy your face while you work out there is no elsewhere.

      Your salvage and your gems and whatever painting you cut out of a dead man's wall will fetch about half of what you've been telling people. Take it. It cost you nothing but the friends.

      There's no sheriff here, no magistrate, and not one trial in living memory. Keep the peace and you can drink beside the devil himself under any flag you please. Break it and you'll learn how quick two hundred people can come to a verdict, and what they keep the rope for.
