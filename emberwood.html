The Last Village on the Road

Emberwood

You walked the last five miles of Champion's Way expecting a graveyard. You're fifteen years early, and the ale's still cold.

By Tibbs Mallory, Our Man in the Mud

In Short

Emberwood is the last village on Champion's Way, about five miles south of Drakkenheim, and the only safe place nearby to rest, heal, and resupply. Gear costs double what it does elsewhere, food five times as much, and salvage sells for about half its value. There is no law here but the villagers themselves, and each of the five factions keeps a contact in town.

Here's Emberwood the way I tell it to anybody buying. Before the Fall it was a market, a pub, and a great many pine trees, and nobody outside the county could have found it with a map and a guide. Then the stars came down on the capital up the road and blew out every window in town. In the years after, the fields went grey, the pines started dying from the top down, and most of the families packed up and left. About two hundred stayed. Mean, stubborn sons of bitches, proud of both, who figured the place was theirs and they'd be damned. Then the adventurers came up the road with coin in their boots, and Emberwood found out what it was for.

What it's for is this. You go north. You come back, if you're able. And between the going and the coming back, the good people of Emberwood relieve you of every copper you own, cheerful as you please. Half the doors in town are nailed shut. What's worth finding is a short walk from the square. What isn't will find you, usually around last call.

Courier Advises: Locals quit learning names after a crew's third trip in. Don't take it personal. Take it as a goal.

A Word on Prices, Before You Complain

How Things Work Here

Nothing grows here. Not a turnip, not a weed. Every nail, loaf, and bandage in town came up that road on a wagon, and whoever drove it wants paying for the trip and for the nerve. So you'll pay double what you paid at home for steel and kit, and five times over for anything you mean to eat. Water goes by the cup, same as ale, and the ale's better. Go on and haggle. The shopkeep will tell you to take your business elsewhere, then lean on the counter and enjoy your face while you work out there isn't any elsewhere. Best entertainment in town, after the Lily.

Going the other way, your salvage and your gems and that painting you cut out of a dead man's parlor will fetch about half of what you've been telling everybody at the bar. Take the money. It only cost you the friends.

There's no sheriff in Emberwood. No magistrate, no jail, and no trial anybody can remember. That surprises people for about a day. Keep the peace and you can drink elbow to elbow with a Lantern, a pilgrim, a mage, and a cutthroat under any flag you like, and folks do, nightly. Break it, and you'll see how fast two hundred people can reach a verdict with no judge to slow them down. Ask what they keep the rope for. On second thought, don't.

Do Not Rest in the Haze

A Public Notice

Do not rest in the Haze. Do not nap in the Haze. Do not “sit down for just a moment” in the Haze. Do not lie back “to look at the pretty colors.” The Courier has run this notice in every issue for fifteen years, and in every issue somebody reads it, nods, and goes and does it anyway.

The Flame Still Burns

Healing, blessings, and prayers for the departed, at any hour, for an offering. Flamekeeper Hanna welcomes all who come in good faith and most who don't.
