    The City That Was

    A short history of Drakkenheim, for those who missed it.

    For a century and a half Drakkenheim was the crown of Westemär: a hundred thousand souls packed among spires, cathedrals, academies, and fat merchant houses along the Drann. House von Kessel ran it all from Castle Drakken, up on its crag over the north ward. The last of that line, King Ulrich IV, was handed a quiet kingdom and gave his reign over to architecture, schooling, and pleasures this paper won't print.

    On the sixteenth of September, 1111, the sky opened. Those who lived through it in the outer wards will tell you of a light so bright it threw shadows backward, and a sound they still hear when the wind turns. The dust never came down. A glittering mist rolled through the streets and stayed, crystals came up out of the cobbles like frost, and whoever stopped too long got changed. The royal family was home that night.

    How a Kingdom Broke Itself

    Brother Against Sister

    King Ulrich had a younger brother and sister, Mannfred and Cecilia von Kessel, and neither of them was in the city the night it died, which is the only luck that family ever had. For three years the pair spent fortunes together, paying priests of the Flame and mages of the Academy to find out what had become of the king. Every scrying came back as noise. They paid for the failed expeditions too, and damn near beggared themselves at it.

    At last Mannfred quit on the city and announced he'd take the crown himself and move the capital someplace the air didn't glitter. Cecilia said no. Nobody had found a body. The nobility split down the middle, and from 1114 to 1119 they settled it with armies. Ask any veteran in Emberwood which side they marched for and watch their face shut like a door.

    Westemär Today

    A kingdom on paper. Its barons, counts, and dukes run their own dirt and nurse old grudges over which von Kessel they backed, though with the coffers empty and the sons in the ground it seldom comes to blows now. Still, mind your mouth about the war in mixed company. Half the room fought on the other side.

    Have You Seen These Royals?

    The Empty Throne

    Tap a poster to turn it over.

    Somewhere up in the ruin of Castle Drakken stands the Throne of Westemär, and no one has put a backside on it in fifteen years. Every noble in the kingdom wants it filled, mostly by themselves. The trouble is proof.

    Anybody can claim royal blood, and a ring or a letter settles nothing when you can have one forged in Emberwood for the price of a meal. A true claim has to be backed by something from inside the city, which is why the noble houses keep paying for the Hooded Lanterns, and why every outfit in the ruins has one eye on that castle.
