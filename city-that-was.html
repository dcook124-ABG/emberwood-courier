From the Archive

The City That Was

A short history of Drakkenheim, for those who missed it, and for those who were there and would rather not discuss it.

By Wendeline Hask, Keeper of the Courier's Morgue

A newspaper's archive is called its morgue. In Emberwood, the confusion is understandable.

In Short

On 16 September 1111, falling stars destroyed Drakkenheim, the capital of Westemär, and the royal family vanished. The king's brother and sister fought a civil war over the throne from 1114 to 1119, and both died. The throne has stood empty ever since.

For a century and a half, Drakkenheim was the crown of Westemär. A hundred thousand people lived among its spires, cathedrals, academies, and fat merchant houses along the River Drann. House von Kessel ruled it all from Castle Drakken, high on its crag over the north ward. The last of the line to hold the throne, King Ulrich IV, inherited a quiet kingdom and spent his reign on architecture, schooling, and certain pleasures this paper will not print. (The Morgue has them on file. The Morgue is not lending.)

On the sixteenth of September, 1111, the sky opened. Those who lived through it in the outer wards describe a light so bright it threw shadows the wrong way, and a sound some of them still hear when the wind turns. Then the dust that ought to have settled simply didn't. A glittering mist rolled through the streets and stayed. Crystal came up through the cobbles like frost. Whoever lingered too long began to change. The royal family was home that night. All of them.

From the Morgue: The clock in the old print shop stopped at 8:13 that evening, when the window came in. The Editor has never had it fixed. He says it's the most accurate thing in the building.

Fifteen Years, Year by Year

From the Fall to Your Arrival

  1. 1111

    The Stars Fall

    At thirteen minutes past eight on the evening of the sixteenth of September, a storm of falling stars comes down on Drakkenheim with the whole royal family at home.

  2. 1112

    An Army Dies in the Mist

    The crown sends an army to take back the capital. Thousands die in the Haze. The few who stagger out are raving, and they don't last long.

  3. 1113

    A Prophecy Published

    Lucretia Mathias predicted the Fall years ahead and was silenced for it. Now she expands her writings into the Testament of the Falling Fire, and this time people read them.

  4. 1114

    Brother Against Sister

    Two more expeditions go into the mist and come to nothing, and the quarrel between Mannfred and Cecilia von Kessel over the empty throne turns into a war.

  5. 1115

    Some Come Back

    With the armies busy killing each other, adventurers try the ruins for plunder. A few come back alive, with stories of monsters and samples of a strange crystal.

  6. 1116

    A Pilgrim at the Crater

    Lucretia Mathias walks to the Crater's Edge on her own two feet and carries what she found there back to Elyria.

  7. 1117

    Delerium Is Named

    The Amethyst Academy starts paying for what folks have been calling meteor shards, and gives the crystal the name that stuck.

  8. 1119

    The War Runs Out of Claimants

    Assassins kill Mannfred von Kessel and his children. Days later Cecilia is dead too, of causes no physician will name. With nobody left to fight for, the war simply stops.

  9. 1120

    Cast Out

    The Divine Matriarch excommunicates Lucretia Mathias for heresy. Mathias goes back to Drakkenheim, which is not the punishment anyone had in mind.

  10. 1121

    Emberwood Booms

    Adventurers come up the road in numbers, and a village that had been quietly dying finds itself the favorite stop of every treasure hunter and cutthroat in the realm.

  11. 1123

    The Lanterns Enter the City

    The 4th Provisional Expeditionary Force to Reclaim the Capital makes its first forays into the ruins. Within a season, everybody is calling them the Hooded Lanterns.

  12. 1124

    A Gate Is Taken

    The Lanterns dig in at the old Shepherd's Gate and learn that taking a gate is the easy part.

  13. 1126

    You Arrive

    Fifteen years on, the city's dead and the money isn't. You've come about the money.

How a Kingdom Broke Itself

Brother Against Sister

King Ulrich had a younger brother and sister, Mannfred and Cecilia von Kessel. Neither was in the city the night it fell, which was the last stroke of luck that family ever had. For three years the two of them spent fortunes side by side, paying priests of the Flame and mages of the Academy to learn what had become of the king. Every scrying came back as noise. They paid for the failed expeditions too, and very nearly beggared themselves doing it.

In the end Mannfred gave up on the city. He announced he would take the crown himself and move the capital somewhere the air didn't glitter. Cecilia refused. Nobody had found a body. The nobility split down the middle, and from 1114 to 1119 they settled the matter with armies. Ask a veteran in Emberwood which side they marched for, and watch their face shut like a door.

From the Morgue: The Courier covered the war from start to finish and never once printed which side the Editor favored. When asked, he says, “The one that paid for its advertisements.”

Westemär Today

A kingdom on paper. Its barons, counts, and dukes rule their own patches of dirt and nurse old grudges over which von Kessel they backed. With the treasuries empty and the sons in the ground, it seldom comes to blows anymore. Still, mind your tongue about the war in mixed company. Half the room fought for the other side, and the other half buried someone who did.

Somewhere up in the ruin of Castle Drakken stands the Throne of Westemär, and no one has put a backside on it in fifteen years. Every noble in the kingdom wants it filled, mostly by themselves. The trouble is proof.

Anybody can claim royal blood, and a ring or a letter settles nothing when you can have one forged in Emberwood for the price of supper. A true claim has to be backed by something from inside the city. That is why the noble houses keep paying for the Hooded Lanterns, and why every outfit in the ruins keeps one eye on that castle.
