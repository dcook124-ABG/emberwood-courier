The Market Report

A Fortune in the Rubble

Opportunities for the brave, the gloved, and the very slightly greedy.

By Barnabas Coyle, Market Desk

In Short

The ruins are full of unclaimed treasure, and delerium is the most valuable thing in them, from about 10 gold for a chip to 5,000 for a geode. Most traders pay about half of those prices; crystals and geodes fetch close to full value. Handle the stones with gloves or tongs.

The market desk reports conditions in the ruins as follows: plentiful supply, eager buyers, and a mortality rate among sellers that the desk declines to print. Drakkenheim was about as rich as a city gets, and the night it died, almost nobody got their valuables out. There are vaults sealed under fallen towers, silver plate still in the sideboards of noble townhouses, and spellbooks on the shelves of mages who won't be back for them. All of it sits in the Haze, the lawful property of people too dead to object.

Then there's the stone. Delerium grows through the ruins, fused into wall and street, humming to itself and drinking up magic the way a sponge drinks water. Mages will pay very nearly anything for it. That makes it the most valuable commodity in Westemär and, in the desk's considered view, the worst one to be caught holding.

Courier Advises: The desk has never met a rich prospector. It has met several prospectors who were rich for an afternoon.

Dealers in Delerium

Aldor the Immense

Trades in stones and stranger wares out of a carriage the size of a chapel in Caravan Court, and pays best to those he likes. Get liked.

Orson Fairweather

Buys delerium and doesn't care where you got it, from an iron-bound stagecoach ringed with armed men who'd as soon you didn't linger.

The Amethyst Academy

Keeps a buyer at the Red Lion Hotel, who is discreet, professional, and has no interest whatever in your opening offer.
