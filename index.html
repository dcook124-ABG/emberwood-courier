From the Editor's Desk

The Big Picture

All you need to know before your first drink, and more than you'll remember after your third.

By Ambrose T. Pell, Editor & Proprietor

Welcome, stranger, to the edge of the grandest ruin in the known world. For a hundred and fifty years House von Kessel ruled the kingdom of Westemär from the city you can see glittering past the pines, and ruled it in comfort. Then, at thirteen minutes past eight on the evening of the sixteenth of September, 1111, the sky split open and rained stars on Drakkenheim. The king, the queen, and every one of their children were home that night. Not one of them has been seen since, alive or otherwise.

What came next, any barfly could have called. The king's brother and sister fell to quarreling over his empty chair, then to raising armies over it, and spent five years bleeding the kingdom white. Both were dead by 1119, and neither ever sat down. Westemär is still a kingdom the way a dropped plate is still a plate, and its capital belongs to whoever can hold a street of it until supper.

But you didn't walk all this way for a history lesson. (The Courier keeps one on hand in the Archive pages anyway, and recommends it warmly.) You came for what the stars left behind. There is a mist up there called the Haze, every color you have a name for and a few you don't, and in fifteen years it has not lifted once. There is a crystal called delerium pushing up through the cobbles like frost through a field, humming to itself, and a stone the size of your fist will buy you a farm. Handle it wrong and it will buy you a funeral. Linger too long in that mist and you'll come back changed, if you come back as anything your mother would claim.

Five great outfits have come to settle what the corpse is for. They will not agree this side of Judgment, and every one of them is desperate for hands it can afford to lose. That, dear reader, is where you come in. Emberwood, last stop on the road, will sell all of you your boots and your beer and your bandages at prices that'll make you weep, and the Courier will be right here writing it all down. Read on. Come back. Buy next week's.
