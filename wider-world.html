    The Wider World

    What lies beyond the road, what they pray to out there, and why they're afraid of their mages.

    By Wendeline Hask, Keeper of the Courier's Morgue

    In Short

    In this world the gods do not answer, holy power comes from devotion, and arcane magic is inherited. The three great realms are Westemär, Elyria, and Caspia, and the Faith of the Sacred Flame is the dominant religion.

    Four Things Strangers Get Wrong

    The gods do not answer.They don't appear, don't speak, and don't meddle. Plenty of things claim to be gods, and the sages can't agree on a damn thing beyond this world.
    Holy power is earned.Clerics, druids, and paladins get there by devotion and resolve, and nobody hands it to them. Break a tenet and nothing happens. Lose your faith and it might.
    Arcane magic is in the blood.Sorcerers and wizards are born mageborn, and no amount of study will make you one. The law bars them from titles and land.
    Great magic is rare.About one soul in a thousand can cast a spell at all. Drakkenheim has lately drawn more than its share, which ought to worry you.

    The Continent

    The Realms

    Westemär

    Deep forests, wide rivers, and rich mines, running north as far as the tundra. Its nobles are proud and its common folk practical, and since the throne went empty its lords have ruled as they please. Drakkenheim was its capital. You're standing in what's left of its front yard.

    Elyria

    A southern peninsula of prairie and river valley, with one of the finest navies on the continent. The Holy City of Lumen is the seat of the Faith of the Sacred Flame, and its Divine Matriarch runs the country in all but name. The knights in the white tabards come from here.

    Caspia

    Rugged country between the Glimmer Mountains and the Diamond Sea, loud with squabbling nobles. Its princes elect a High King every ten years and spend the time in between sticking their noses in their neighbors' business.

    The Free Lands

    Step off the trade roads and the law gives out fast. The dark forests, bogs, and mountains belong to monsters, and to tribes who keep the old ways and have no use for kings.

    The People

    Humans are most of it, though every ancestry turns up on the road to Drakkenheim. Dwarves remember lost kingdoms. Elves live scattered from a homeland none of them can name. The halfling villages round the city stand empty, and nobody in Emberwood likes to say why.

    Trade

    Merchant ships cross the Diamond Sea and the Middle Ocean, and caravans run east to places few have seen. Magic is bought and sold among the rich under Academy rules, and the black market has done very nicely for itself since delerium turned up.

    What People Believe

    Faith and Magic

    The Sacred Flame

    The great faith of the realms, founded a thousand years ago by Saint Tarna, a warlord who became the first paladin. It reveres a divine light rather than a god. Its priests, the Flamekeepers, tend a golden flame in chapels across the continent, including the little one in Emberwood.

    The Old Faith

    A loose pantheon of primal gods, tied up with the elements, the seasons, and the ancestors, with druids for its most common priests. There's a ring of its standing stones just outside Emberwood. Mind your manners there.

    Shadow Cults

    Heretical sects and worshippers of dark, godlike things. The Flame condemns every one of them, though not all of them have earned it, and some have earned a good deal worse.

    The Amethyst Academy

    By law it takes in any child born with magic and trains them until they come of age, and it governs the trade in magical goods. Common folk still fear anyone who casts spells without its colors, and they aren't always wrong to.

    The Edicts of Lumen

    The treaty that ended the wars between mages and the Faith, three hundred years back. It divides power among mages, clergy, and nobles, strips mages of titles and land, binds the Academy to neutrality, and protects mages under the law. Everybody resents some part of it, which is how you know it's working.

    The Schism

    The Fall split the Faith in two. The Silver Order calls delerium a blight. The Followers of the Falling Fire call it a gift. Both of them are armed, and both of them are in Emberwood.

    From the Morgue: Strangers often ask the Morgue which faith is the true one. The Morgue keeps records, not opinions, and notes only that every faith in this camp charges for candles.
